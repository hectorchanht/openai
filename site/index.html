OpenAI / OpenLLM

The flagship that teaches humanity to use AI.

In 2026, using AI is a must-learn for every adult — and for the generation born after AI became the norm, it is survival itself. This app teaches it properly: concepts first.

StatusFlagship — in founding, curriculum canon in build. This page is the live founding record: the end goal, the roadmap, and honest progress, updated as the canon grows.

What it is

Most helplessness in the AI era is not a lack of intelligence. It is structural: problems that stay invisible to the person inside them, because they do not yet have the concept that would make the problem visible. No concept — no sight — no way to learn. So OpenAI / OpenLLM does not start with tricks. It starts by naming the concepts.

Two names, one product. OpenAI faces the public: learning to use AI. OpenLLM goes one layer deeper: learning how large language models themselves work. Every Dawn app uses AI — this one teaches the using. All swords return to one.

And the name is a reclamation: “open” should mean open. Free, open source, an open canon, updated forever.

End goal

A world where no one is locked out of the AI era for lack of a concept — where every adult can direct AI, understand how it works, and spot the structural problems it hides, taught by a canon that never stops updating.

Roadmap — the curriculum

Five dimensions, one canon. Every lesson is built the same way: a world source (papers, experiments, official research), a Dawn specimen (lived proof it was run and tested here), and the concept named.

Dimension 1

Scientific papers, in human words

The transformer, attention, scaling laws — the research that built AI, translated into language anyone can hold.

Dimension 2

Product apps, taken apart

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and the rest — how the products people actually use work, and how to work them well.

Dimension 3

Philosophy — literacy for the AI age

Language games, philosophy of mind, words that make worlds: the concepts underneath the tools.

Dimension 4

Skill cards

Prompting, workflows, verification — and how not to be fooled by AI. Practical cards you can use the same day.

Dimension 5 · Required

The field guide to structural problems

The invisible structures end users cannot see — named, one by one. Each entry: one concept, one picture, one exercise in recognising it. Entry № 1 is the hook — the concept its founder only learned by living it.

A living canon

This is not a course frozen at launch. The canon is fed one-way from the Dawn database and updated perpetually — new findings from the frontier go straight into the curriculum. What is true today stays; what changes, changes here too.

Source A — the world

Papers, experiments and official documentation from across the web. Every claim carries its source and an evidence label — the curriculum's width and verifiability come from here.

Source B — lived proof

Dawn's own record: the walls its founder hit, run through and verified in real work. Not the whole curriculum — its specimens and its evidence.
